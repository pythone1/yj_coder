ABSTRACT
Urban drainage system (UDS) plays a key role in city urbanization, where defective pipes can lead to seepage.
Previous studies have identified the locations of defects in UDS using inverse optimization models. However, the
unique optimal solution neglects uncertainty analysis, which may lead to misdiagnosis. In addition, the multi-
point defect diagnosis has heavy computational burden due to high dimensional parameters space. To address
these issues, this paper proposes a hybrid method that leverages the genetic algorithm (GA) to identify probable
space, and then utilizes the adaptive Metropolis (AM) to provide an estimation of the posterior probability
distribution (PPD). Firstly, a multi-population GA is employed for the maximum exploration within the model
space. Then, AM algorithm is used to explore the final PPD of each pipe defect parameter. The metrics accuracy
( ACC ), Matthews correlation coefficient ( MCC ) and mean absolute error ( MAE ) are used to evaluate the diagnosis
performance. A synthetic UDS case with randomized multi-point seepage scenarios is used to validate the
method. Results indicate that the proposed hybrid method is effective in diagnosing multi-point defect, with
0.91, 0.78 of the hybrid method and 0.87, 0.69 of the DiffeRential Evolution Adaptive Metropolis method for the
ACC and MCC , respectively. Meanwhile, the diagnosis speed has increased by 32 %. The result PPD passes the 90
% confidence interval validation. The proposed method can provide effective uncertainty analysis to reduce
misdiagnosis of the traditional method.
1. Introduction
Complex urban drainage system (UDS) plays a key role in city rapid
urbanization development ( Luo et al., 2024 ). A scientific UDS signifi-
cantly contributes to urban flood control ( Karn et al., 2023 ; Lund et al.,
2019 ). However, especially in developing countries, for the long years of
UDS construction, unscientific operation, materials aging and external
disturbance, existing pipes have suffered a lot of damage, leading to
defects ( Kumar et al., 2018 ; Tariq et al., 2022 ). Sewage leakage, flow
seepage, groundwater infiltration and pollution will occur with defec-
tive pipes ( Grimmeisen et al., 2017 ; Harvey and McBean, 2014 ; Liu
et al., 2018 ). For example, studies have shown that water loss caused by
defective aging pipe systems can reach up to 50 % ( Kanakoudis and
Muhammetoglu, 2014 ). Municipal authorities often employ a passive
strategy, waiting to conduct repairs until defects cause significant
failures ( Rokstad and Ugarelli, 2016 ). As a result, with the increasing
urban population and environmental pressure, the failure caused by
defective UDS requires significant manpower, financial resources and
material resources to repair, even not thoroughly addressing the un-
derlying defects. Thus, large budgets have been devoted to conduct
preventive maintenance work by municipalities ( Sinha and Knight,
2004 ). Specifically, the issue of timely defect monitoring is of great
importance for UDS.
Closed circuit television (CCTV) is so far the most common method to
evaluate the defects of pipe networks worldwide ( Nguyen et al., 2021 ).
However, such manual interpretation of inspection images or videos is
time-consuming and labour-intensive. Limited by technical threshold
and equipment costs, CCTV is not suitable for large spatial scales and
high temporal frequency inspections ( Blackwood et al., 2005 ). Thus,
diagnosis methods based on model approaches are widely applied

Unlike the use of high precision pressure monitoring equipment on
WDS ( Gupta and Kulat, 2018 ; Huang et al., 2020 ; Qi et al., 2018 ), the
flow in UDS is typically characterized as a non-pressure flow. Thus, the
diagnosis model-based method of UDS often relies on more compre-
hensive simulation. For example, the storm water management model

for inverse model diagnosis. Through a trial-and-error process, the in-
verse model determines the parameters that most closely match the
monitoring values, thus obtaining the defect diagnosis results. Inverse
model based on optimization methods is a common approach in existing
research. Xu et al. (2016) used water balance and chemical mass balance
method to estimate the sewage inflow into the stormwater network,
which successfully determined the defects and illicit connections of
pipes. Zhao et al. (2020) optimized the specific infiltration locations by
an inverse model with the support of chemical tracers and optimization
model, which identified groundwater infiltration in success. Xu et al.
(2021) coupled a genetic algorithm (GA) with SWMM to track the illicit
connections of sewer system, which provided data basis for pipe
reconstruction and rehabilitation.
However, the objective of obtaining a “ single ” optimal solution by
optimization algorithms does not align with the inherent nature of UDS
pipe diagnosis. In fact, the diagnosis process should encompass uncer-
tainty, such as monitoring data uncertainty ( Shao et al., 2021 ) and
model simulation uncertainty ( Luo et al., 2023 ). From the start to the
end, the uncertainty accompanies the inverse model for diagnosis ( Wang
and Harrison, 2013 ). In this regard, the posterior probability distribu-
tion (PPD) obtained by uncertainty analysis methods ( Pan et al., 2019 ),
such as Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) series methods, instead of
the optimal solution of the optimization algorithm, contains more in-
formation for diagnosis.
So far, MCMC methods have been successfully applied to inverse
problem. For example, the Metropolis-Hastings (MH) algorithm
( Hastings, 1970 ), is suitable for any situation where the relative density
of the target distribution can be calculated. Wang et al. (2014, 2013)
utilized the MCMC method to devise sampling strategies for WDS to
ascertain the optimal sensor placement locations. Wu et al. (2020)
coupled the Bayesian inference theory with a surrogate model, which
was constructed to determine the source of sudden water pollution in-
cidents in rivers and lakes. Shao et al. (2021) established a
Bayesian-SWMM coupled stochastic model to reconstruct the complete
profile of an unknown discharging incidence.
Unlike aforementioned issues, the diagnosis of UDS with multi-point
defects is an inverse problem in a high-dimensional space. Due to the
uncertainty in the number of defects and the extent of the damage, the
computational workload becomes a burden. Limited by an appropriate
proposal distribution, and in high-dimensional spaces, MH method
convergence can be difficult. To address the issue, adaptive Metropolis
(AM) was proposed ( Andrieu and Thoms, 2008 ). Through automatically
optimized by adjustment based on the sampling history, AM sampling
can perform better in convergence. In addition, to enhance the efficiency
of MCMC sampling, evolution algorithms are often integrated. For
example, Yang et al. (2016) combined differential evolution algorithm
and MH algorithm to identify multi-point sudden water pollution sour-
ces. To ensure the reliability of the final probability estimates, the in-
tegrated evolution algorithms must maximize the exploration of the
parameter space. The multi-population GA has been proven to possess
the ability to maximize the exploration of the parameter space ( Aleardi
and Mazzotti, 2017 ). By appropriately structuring the population size
and establishing competition and migration mechanisms,
multi-population GA can effectively mitigate genetic drift and thor-
oughly explore the parameter space ( Pourvaziri and Naderi, 2014 ; Sun
et al., 2022 ). However, there is currently a lack of researches employing
uncertainty analysis methods for diagnosing multi-point defective pipes
in UDS. Moreover, the study of combining AM with multi-population GA
to enhance the efficiency of uncertainty diagnosis is still not available.
The aim of this study is to enhance performance of multi-point defect
diagnosis in UDS. The innovation of this study is diagnosing multi-defect
in UDS using uncertainty analysis methods. The proposed method can
provide effective uncertainty analysis to reduce misdiagnosis of the
traditional method. In addition, a hybrid method has been proposed,
broadly exploring the parameter space through a multi-population GA,

3.2. The hybrid diagnosis method
A hybrid method containing GA and AM algorithms is proposed to
take the advantages of both. Fig. 3 illustrates the method ’ s flowchart. A
brief description of this hybrid two-step method is as follows:
(1) Multiple populations will be generated for a multi-population GA.
The migration and competition among populations are carried
out at fixed intervals. Upon reaching the designated number of
evolutionary generations, the resulting new population forms the
initial PPD.
(2) Utilizing the initial PPD as a form of prior information, AM
sampling is conducted, resulting in the determination of the PPD
of the parameters.
Further technical details will be provided in the following sections.
3.2.1. Step 1: multi-population genetic algorithm
Traditional GA are computationally efficient but often suffer from
genetic drift effect ( Rogers and Prugel-Bennett, 1999 ), leading to being
stuck in local optimum and struggling to explore the parameter space in
depth. To address the issue, a multi-population GA is employed for
searching the initial PPD. The multi-population mechanism ensures the
diversification capability of the algorithm ( Pourvaziri and Naderi,
2014 ). Multiple populations will be generated in different parameter
spaces. They will undergo evolutionary iterations within each popula-
tion following the typical GA crossover and mutation operations.
Meanwhile, at regular intervals, competition and migration occur
among the populations. Upon reaching the designated number of
evolutionary generations, the individuals from all independent pop-
ulations are merged, and inferior individuals are removed based on the
roulette wheel selection method. In the implementation of the method, a
total of five populations are generated, each consisting of 80 individuals.
The evolutionary process spans 25 generations, with population
competition and migration occurring every five generations.
The individual in the population has a chromosome with genes
(represents the defect level value of each pipe). These genes are set as the
pipe parameters in the SWMM. The length of gene in the GA is decided
by the number of pipes in UDS. The choice of objective function is
crucial to the performance of algorithm. Due to the presence of multiple

3.3. Generation of synthetic scenarios
To simulate multiple scenarios of multi-point defects, four groups
sample data is generated randomly by PySWMM. The data is listed in the
Table 1 . Each group has 100 samples. To detect the accuracy of multi-
point seepage diagnosis, the number of seepage pipe ranges from 1 to
5 with 20 samples of each number. Specific seepage pipe index is
determined by non-release sampling. SRR is assumed to follow to a
uniform distribution between 0.1 and 1.0. Simulation error and moni-
toring error are significant sources of uncertainty in defect diagnosis.
Errors are simulated by adding noise. Referring to prior research, the
noise follows a normal distribution N ( 0 , σ ) , with the standard deviation σ
reflecting the level of noise. Existing studies typically use σ values be-
tween 0.01 and 0.05 ( Shao et al., 2021 ; Yang et al., 2016 ). To reflect the
impact of varying degrees of error, the standard deviation σ is set to
0.01, 0.02 or 0.05. To analyze the impact of the number of monitoring
sites, control group 4 is set with 1 site while group 1, 2 and 3 have 3 sites.
3.4. Evaluation metrics
Although uncertainty analysis methods often yield PPD, it remains
necessary to evaluate the effectiveness using statistical indicators. Due
to the parameter value being zero (intact) or greater than zero (defec-
tive), the distribution is likely to be skewed. The mean can be heavily
influenced by extreme values, leading to a misleading representation of
the diagnosis. For reflecting the typical defect severity by being robust to
outliers and providing a clearer picture of the distribution center, the
median is used to represent the final estimation of the PPD.

4.1. Comparison of hybrid method with original method
4.1.1. Multi-population GA versus standard GA
The self-organizing map (SOM) ( Kalteh et al., 2008 ; Kohonen, 2013 )
method is used to demonstrate the advantages of the proposed GA. As
shown in Fig. 4 , a 7 × 7 hexagonal grid, comprising a total of 49 neurons,
is utilized in the SOM network. Employing a hexagonal grid, implies that
each neuron node is closest in distance to the six surrounding adjacent
nodes. Although SOM, as an unsupervised method, yields different re-
sults after each training session, the resulting cluster distribution dia-
gram can still reflect the distribution of all individuals in the parameter
space within the GA. Clearly, after only 25 generations of evolution the
standard GA individuals have already clustered in several parts of the
space. As a contrast, the multi-population GA is still attempting to
explore the entire parameter space at this point. When it comes to the
point of 50 generations, the standard GA may have fallen into a possible
local optimum, evidencing the genetic drift effect

4.2. Performance of synthetic scenarios
4.2.1. Impacts of the number of seepage pipes
In the effectiveness of multi-point seepage diagnosis, 1 to 5 seepage
pipes of synthetic scenarios are considered in the study work. The
metrics results are shown in the Fig. 6 . The ACC metric decreases with
the increase of seepage pipes, which decreases from 0.99 in 1 pipe

same pattern. Different from the ACC metric, except the dataset G1, with
the change of number of seepage pipes, there is no obvious change
pattern of the MCC metric. For example, from 1 pipe to 5 pipes, in
dataset G2, the mean values of MCC metric are 0.55, 0.69, 0.59, 0.66,

metric difference.
The MAE results are shown in Fig. 7 . Like the ACC and MCC metrics,
with the increase of seepage pipes and monitoring error, the metric MAE
increases. However, in the experimental results of G1, G2 and G3

provide a relatively accurate assessment of pipe damage level.

4.3. The validation of uncertainty diagnosis result PPD
Although the evaluation metrics ACC , MCC , and MAE can charac-
terize the effectiveness of the proposed method within the sample range,
the more crucial objective for individual diagnosis cases is to derive the
defect PPD for all pipes within the case. Therefore, the accuracy of the
PPD must first be demonstrated. As shown in Fig. 10 , this study selects a
sample from the G2 dataset, where the MCC metric performed the worst.
The ACC and MCC of this case are 0.33 and 0.19, respectively. To verify
the effectiveness, the PPD, along with a different observed 3-hour
rainfall event, is inputted into the UDS model. The resulting outlet
flow is outputted within the probability simulation, passing the 90 %
confidence interval validation with coverage to non-coverage ratio
being 11:1. This indicates that the PPD obtained from the uncertainty
analysis diagnosis is effective, and the results can simultaneously guide
the calibration of the UDS model, future simulation and prediction
efforts.
Additionally, as shown in Fig. 11 , the PPD for all pipe SRR is fully
mapped. It is known that more valuable information can be discovered
from the PPD to aid in decision-making. The case includes two actual
defect locations, both of which are successfully diagnosed. Furthermore,
the diagnosis values are nearly identical to the true values, and the
probability peak values of the PPD are also concentrated at the true
values. This indicates that the proposed method effectively converges to
the damage locations when diagnosing significant defects. The primary
reason for the lower evaluation metrics is the high number of FP diag-
nosis pipes, totaling 12. However, by prioritizing inspections based on
probability, one can avoid the pitfall of optimal solution errors in the
diagnosis process, thereby enhancing the efficiency of diagnosis.
4.4. Advantages and limitations
4.4.1. Form of defects
In this study, a defect parameter seepage rate is used to characterize
the seepage condition of defective pipes. Compared to existing studies
( Xu et al., 2021 ), the parameter does not require additional time pa-
rameters for changes in seepage volume, more accurately reflecting the
actual flow process in pipes. The parameter significantly simplifies the
sampling complexity of the inverse problem. In fact, many other forms of
defect can also be characterized by such simple parameters. For
instance, in pipes that experience blockage, the condition can be
modeled by using a "blockage rate" to limit the maximum allowable
flow, effectively simulating the blockage scenario in the UDS opera-
tional model. However, a limitation of this study is that the case only
considers UDS pipes with a single defect parameter. In actual diagnosis
cases, multiple defect forms often occur in combination. The uncertainty
of these defect forms should also be a focus of future research. Although
multiple defect forms increase parameter dimensions, certain data
encoding methods can circumvent or mitigate this issue (e.g., variables
greater than 0 indicate seepage, while those less than 0 indicate
blockage).

uncertainty analysis results. Thanks to the robust exploratory

4.4.3. Case study
The research case in this study is drawn from an actual UDS.
Although covering only 0.081 km
2
with 19 pipes, the case still repre-
sents a complete drainage system of a school and a fundamental unit in a
large UDS. This case study does not seem to demonstrate the application
of the proposed method on a city-scale UDS. However, even a more
complex large-scale UDS can be decomposed by using complex network
theory tools ( Simone et al., 2022 ; Yang et al., 2017 ). Thus, block diag-
nosis can be achieved by decomposing the large UDS into unit UDSs.
Since the outlets of these small UDSs often converging into a main
drainage pipe, a segmented diagnosis of the large UDS can be achieved
by diagnosing the small UDS linked to the main pipe. This
decomposition-based diagnosis strategy is more efficient. Future
research can focus on decomposition strategies and the efficiency of
parallel diagnosis.
5. Conclusions
Pipe diagnosis is key to enhancing the efficiency of UDS mainte-
nance. A hybrid method, combining the rapid spatial exploration
capability of multi-population GA with the uncertainty analysis capa-
bility of AM sampling, is developed to diagnose the multi-point defective
pipes. This method conducts through SWMM and outputs the PPD of
defect level parameter for each pipe in the UDS. The effectiveness of
diagnosing seepage is demonstrated through case studies. The follow-
ings are the major findings:
(1) The proposed hybrid method outperforms the DREAM method in
diagnosing multi-point defects. The mean ACC and MCC values for
the hybrid method are 0.91 and 0.78, respectively, showing an
improvement compared to 0.87 and 0.69 for the DREAM method.
Through extensive exploration of the parameter space using multi-
population GA, the diagnosis speed has increased by 32 %. In the
synthetic samples with three monitoring stations, the mean ACC and
MCC range from 0.93 to 0.75 and from 0.63 to 0.47, respectively.
Similarly, the mean MAE ranges from 0.16 to 0.23. With increasing
monitoring error, the mean ACC and MCC decrease from 0.91 to 0.82
and from 0.79 to 0.44, respectively, while the mean MAE increases
from 0.19 to 0.20.
(2) The PPD can provide accurate probabilistic UDS simulation with
defective status. By inputting PPD and a new observed rainfall into
the UDS model, the resulting outlet flow validation passes the 90 %
confidence interval validation. Even with a sample of ACC and MCC
values of only 0.33 and 0.19, respectively, through probabilistic
detection, the diagnosis can avoid the pitfall of optimal solution er-
rors, thereby enhancing diagnosis efficiency.
Future research will focus on multiple forms of defects diagnosis,
surrogate model diagnosis and large UDS decomposition diagnosis.
