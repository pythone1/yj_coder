ABSTRACT
Urban drainage system (UDS) plays a key role in city urbanization, where defective pipes can lead to seepage.
Previous studies have identified the locations of defects in UDS using inverse optimization models. However, the
unique optimal solution neglects uncertainty analysis, which may lead to misdiagnosis. In addition, the multi-
point defect diagnosis has heavy computational burden due to high dimensional parameters space. To address
these issues, this paper proposes a hybrid method that leverages the genetic algorithm (GA) to identify probable
space, and then utilizes the adaptive Metropolis (AM) to provide an estimation of the posterior probability
distribution (PPD). Firstly, a multi-population GA is employed for the maximum exploration within the model
space. Then, AM algorithm is used to explore the final PPD of each pipe defect parameter. The metrics accuracy
( ACC ), Matthews correlation coefficient ( MCC ) and mean absolute error ( MAE ) are used to evaluate the diagnosis
performance. A synthetic UDS case with randomized multi-point seepage scenarios is used to validate the
method. Results indicate that the proposed hybrid method is effective in diagnosing multi-point defect, with
0.91, 0.78 of the hybrid method and 0.87, 0.69 of the DiffeRential Evolution Adaptive Metropolis method for the
ACC and MCC , respectively. Meanwhile, the diagnosis speed has increased by 32 %. The result PPD passes the 90
% confidence interval validation. The proposed method can provide effective uncertainty analysis to reduce
misdiagnosis of the traditional method.

3.3. Generation of synthetic scenarios
To simulate multiple scenarios of multi-point defects, four groups
sample data is generated randomly by PySWMM. The data is listed in the
Table 1 . Each group has 100 samples. To detect the accuracy of multi-
point seepage diagnosis, the number of seepage pipe ranges from 1 to
5 with 20 samples of each number. Specific seepage pipe index is
determined by non-release sampling. SRR is assumed to follow to a
uniform distribution between 0.1 and 1.0. Simulation error and moni-
toring error are significant sources of uncertainty in defect diagnosis.
Errors are simulated by adding noise. Referring to prior research, the
noise follows a normal distribution N ( 0 , σ ) , with the standard deviation σ
reflecting the level of noise. Existing studies typically use σ values be-
tween 0.01 and 0.05 ( Shao et al., 2021 ; Yang et al., 2016 ). To reflect the
impact of varying degrees of error, the standard deviation σ is set to
0.01, 0.02 or 0.05. To analyze the impact of the number of monitoring
sites, control group 4 is set with 1 site while group 1, 2 and 3 have 3 sites.
3.4. Evaluation metrics
Although uncertainty analysis methods often yield PPD, it remains
necessary to evaluate the effectiveness using statistical indicators. Due
to the parameter value being zero (intact) or greater than zero (defec-
tive), the distribution is likely to be skewed. The mean can be heavily
influenced by extreme values, leading to a misleading representation of
the diagnosis. For reflecting the typical defect severity by being robust to
outliers and providing a clearer picture of the distribution center, the
median is used to represent the final estimation of the PPD.
